# 0041: Workspace file-system calls run in one bounded pool guarded by mount, and folder watches are a poll

- Status: Proposed (2026-11-15), with the F4 design revision 3; not approved. New in revision 2, in answer to the review of revision 1 (findings B4, M1, M2, M10, m11). Revised in revision 3 after the delta review of revision 2 (findings N-B1, N-M4, minors 6 and 11): the bound is per system call, calls are charged by device, a path is marked before a mount, marks re-check what they hid, and the pool is a value. Revised in revision 4 after the delta review of revision 3 (R3-M1, R3-M2, R3-M4): a parked call keeps its slot, so a mount parks at most 4 threads; a descriptor budget; token-based static rules. Revised in revision 5 (R4-M1 and minors): coalesced waiters hold no thread; a probe before a volume is marked; the budget counted by threads; an allowlist for the static rules. The number is provisional (see the README's numbering notes).
- Context:
  - F4 WS-9 AC3: every file-system call F4 makes for one root or one `ACCESS.md` runs off the UI thread under a 2-second bound, and a root that does not answer never delays the others by more than that bound. WS-9 AC6: no recursive watch of a root; at most 500 watches per window.
  - A hub's checkouts are paths a file names. They can be on a network mount that is dead, and a blocked `stat` on a hard mount cannot be cancelled: its thread stays blocked until the kernel answers.
  - Revision 1 ran each call on its own thread and stopped every call once 32 were blocked. Revision 2 bounded each job at 2 seconds and marked its mount stuck when a job ran over. Its delta review showed: a job of many fast calls (200 index files, a 1 MiB read, a 500-folder tick) passed 2 seconds on a live mount and marked it; jobs touched mounts they never declared (a link followed by `open`, `entry.metadata()` on a mount-point child, links inside `.git`); on macOS the home folder, the hubs and most checkouts share one volume, so one slow job turned every local root "not answering"; nothing was re-checked when a mark lifted; and the pool was process-wide, so parallel tests shared it.
  - macOS firmlinks: `/Users` and other folders on the system volume at `/` are firmlinks into the data volume at `/System/Volumes/Data`; `lstat` reports a folder and `realpath` keeps the `/Users` spelling, so the longest mount-point prefix of a home-folder path is `/`, not the volume the call enters. Checked by reading Apple's documented layout, not run.
  - `notify` 7.0.0's `fsevent.rs` restarts the one FSEvents stream on every `watch()` and `unwatch()` (lines 283 to 297), filters `NonRecursive` itself over a stream that delivers whole subtrees (lines 524 to 541), and reports canonical paths (line 367).
  - Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-15-workspaces-design.md`, sections 6, 7 and 11.5.
- Decision:
  - **One pool, as a value.** `workspace::bounded::Bounded` (8 workers, 8 spares, 4 slots per mount for calls in flight and parked together, identical `fstatat` and `readlinkat` calls coalesced (a waiter gives up its worker and descriptors, waits as a continuation of the first call, and resumes with its answer or with "not answering" when it is declared blocked; no waiter is ever parked), its own watchdog and mount-table refresher) is built by the app and held in Tauri managed state, and passed to every function that touches a hub's paths. Tests build their own. No process-wide state in `workspace/` or `pathkey.rs`. Hang seams release on drop.
  - **The bound is per system call: 2 seconds from when the call is issued.** Only a call blocked past it marks anything. Each job also has a deadline for its answer (2 seconds for a root, an `ACCESS.md`, a listing, the finished-hub check; a tick has none); a job past its deadline answers "not answering" and marks nothing. A job queued again under another mount keeps its deadline.
  - **Every call is made through a descriptor of the folder already resolved (`fstatat`, `openat` with `O_NOFOLLOW`, `readlinkat`), so no call follows a link,** and each call is charged to the file system it enters: to a child's own mount when the child is a mount point in the mount table (matched on macOS in its listed spelling and without `/System/Volumes/Data`), else to the device of the folder it is made in. Listings take names and `d_type` from `readdir` and sizes from `fstatat`, charging a mount-point child to its own mount. Nothing inside `.git` is followed.
  - **A path first, then a mount.** A blocked call marks its path; calls at or below it answer at once. A parked call keeps its slot, and the mount is marked when the blocked path is its mount point, or when all 4 slots are parked and a path-based probe of its mount point, on a thread outside the pool, does not answer in 2 seconds; so a mount parks at most 4 workers and one probe thread. A probe that answers means a live volume with slow reads (a cloud file being downloaded, suspected): no mark, a transient slow-down of that volume, stated. (Revision 3 freed a parked call's slot, so a first wave blocked below a mount point let a second wave start: 8 threads for one mount.)
  - **Descriptors.** No descriptor is held by a queued job (it carries the folder as text with its device and inode and re-opens and checks it on resume); a pool thread, running or parked, holds at most 2 (a late `openat` return has its descriptor closed and its permit released); a watch tick opens and closes each parent within its read; probes hold none. F4 holds at most 33 (16 threads × 2 + 1), enforced by a semaphore. `EMFILE` answers "not answering" and marks nothing. The process's soft limit (256 for a Dock-started app; Rust's standard library does not raise it) is not raised by F4.
  - **Static rules, by tokens.** A small lexer in a test module (no new dependency) checks an allowlist of the paths the modules may name, with per-file additions only for the named `Fs` implementation files, the mount table and the record file, and no disk-touching `Path` method in either call form, that no process-wide state exists (`static` items, `OnceLock`, `LazyLock`, `OnceCell`, `Once`, `once_cell`, `lazy_static`, `thread_local`, `Box::leak`), and that nothing logs; a planted file per rule proves it fails.
  - **Recovery and re-checking.** A mark lifts when a parked call under it returns or its mount leaves the table. Then every open hub's state, roots and files shown "not answering" under it are checked again and re-published, and watch sets re-add their folders. There is no timer.
  - **Queues** are per mount, round-robin by submitter, with no cap that refuses; each open job keeps at most 16 jobs queued.
  - **Caps on work.** Discovery checks at most 500 candidate rows per hub and reads at most 201 files, and is cut 10 seconds after the finished-hub check.
  - **Opening streams.** The open job publishes a first view at once and better views as roots are checked, each under a higher `gen`; its last view is `final`.
  - **Commands never block an async worker.** Every F4 command is `async` and works inside `tauri::async_runtime::spawn_blocking`.
  - **Folder watches are a poll.** A workspace view sets no operating-system watch. Each window has a set of at most 500 folders whose modification time is read every 2 seconds while the window is visible, one tick per mount, each read a call with its own bound.
- Consequences:
  - A slow but live mount marks nothing. A dead mount parks at most 4 threads and is marked once its mount point or its fourth slot blocks; a dead share at a mount point the table missed holds one slot of the volume above it (coalescing) and marks only its own path.
  - Each dead mount parks at most 4 threads, each missed share 1. With two dead mounts the 8 spares are used; a third takes 4 of the 8 workers; with four dead mounts at once all 16 threads are parked and F4's file reads answer "not answering" until a mark lifts. A hard-mounted share that never answers keeps its threads parked until it is unmounted or ADE restarts. Both are stated, not solved. Nothing outside F4 uses the pool.
  - That `statfs.f_fsid.val[0]` equals `st_dev` on macOS, the data-volume spelling of mount points below a firmlink, and that `getfsstat(MNT_NOWAIT)` never blocks are not verified; a mismatch in the first only splits a mount's limit of 4, the second is caught by the path mark, and the third would freeze the snapshot only. `macos_only` tests pin the firmlink charging and a real mount point.
  - Watches are non-recursive by nature, the same on every platform, and tested on Linux CI. A change shows up to 2 seconds late. A window with a workspace view in sight makes up to 500 `stat` calls every 2 seconds.
  - A change to a file's content does not change its folder's modification time. S2's live refresh must also poll the `ACCESS.md` files themselves.
  - New code that reads a hub or a checkout uses this pool, through its context, and does not start its own threads or call `std::fs` on those paths.
  - Rejected: a thread per call with a global cap (revision 1); a bound per job (revision 2: marks a live mount); charging by the longest mount-point prefix (revision 2: wrong under firmlinks); marking the mount on the first blocked call (a missed mount point would mark the startup volume); guarding by folder only (siblings each hang separately); `notify` with `NonRecursive`; a recursive `notify` watch with a filter; kqueue (one descriptor per folder against launchd's soft limit of 256 open files).
