# Alerts UX: an audit of today's alerts, a restyle, and optional follow-ups (draft 4)

The owner asked on 2026-11-18 to "fix the ADE alerts", named three (**an agent
needs you**, **an agent finished**, **the context and token toasts**) and one
complaint: **look and feel** (placement, wording, size, styling).

Status: **proposal, not approved.** Draft 1 was blocked in adversarial review
(7 blockers, 8 majors, 11 minors). Draft 2 answered those and followed the
owner's two decisions of 2026-11-18; its delta review found the restyle
blocked on one finding and follow-up F1 on two, with five majors and ten
minors, all of them text and rule fixes. Draft 3 answered each of those
(second table at the end). Its delta check gave: **the restyle (Part B)
passed, with minors**, fit to go to the owner; **follow-up F1 was blocked on
one finding** (D3-B1); twelve minors in all.

**Draft 4 is a wording and rule pass.** The restyle's minors are answered.
F1's blocker is answered and **awaits a re-check**. Nothing is redesigned
and nothing the checks found sound is reopened (third table at the end).
The owner's two decisions stand:

1. **Restyle first.** Part B is a pure restyle that can ship by itself: one
   alert at a time, today's triggers, today's lifetimes, today's ways out.
   Every change to how alerts *behave* is out of Part B and listed in Part C
   as its own optional follow-up. Nothing in Part B depends on Part C.
2. **"Agent finished": both sources, never doubled.** That alert is a
   behaviour change, so it is follow-up F1, fully designed. The restyle only
   restyles and rewords the "finished" toast that exists today.

Out of scope by the owner's instruction, and untouched in every part of this
document: OS notifications (when the attempt is made, how often, its sound
and its words), sound, a Dock badge and an alert history.

Mockup: `2026-11-18-alerts-mockup.html` beside this file (one file, no
network). It has three views: **Today**, **Restyle** and **Follow-ups**.
Names in it are placeholders. It opens on Today; in Restyle the placement
switch starts on AB.

How this was checked: today's behaviour was **read from the code** at
`origin/main` 84389eb; ADE was not run. The mockup was **run** in headless
Chrome. Contrast is **computed** from the theme tokens. See "What was
verified".

## Four things to know first

1. **One toast component makes every alert look the same.** Most of what
   looks wrong follows from that. The restyle fixes it without touching when
   alerts fire.
2. **There is no separate "token toast".** The only alert about tokens is the
   context guard ("Context at 82%").
3. **ADE already raises a "finished" toast, for some agents**, by guessing
   from 3 seconds of silence (`src/hooks/useTerminal.ts:97`). It says "Agent
   completed its task", which it cannot know. The restyle rewords it to what
   is true. F1 adds the real end-of-turn signal where Claude Code provides it.
4. **The restyle alone leaves three known behaviour problems in place**,
   because fixing them changes behaviour: a newer alert still erases the one
   on screen; a needs-you card still stays up after you answer; nothing but
   the mouse reaches a card from the terminal. Each has a follow-up.

## A2. What is wrong with the look and feel

**P1. Every alert looks the same.** No icon, no colour, no kind; all
`role="status"`, errors included.

**P2. Where it sits.** The toast is fixed to the *window*, not to the
terminal. Measured in the mockup's Today view:

- nothing else open: over the newest output lines and the top 24 px of Claude
  Code's input box (not the status line);
- Preview or Board open: almost wholly on that panel, so an alert about a
  terminal sits on a document;
- scratchpad open: on the scratchpad.

The 60 px is a constant; it does not follow the scratchpad or shortcut bar.

**P3. One slot: a newer toast erases the one on screen**, with no motion (the
element is reused, so only the words change). Behaviour; see F3.

**P4. A long name breaks the box; a crafted one rewrites the sentence.** A
78-character name lays out 491 px of text in a 286 px box. Control and
direction characters pass through: a right-to-left override shows `gnp.tab`
as `bat.png`, and nothing marks which words the user typed. The same holds
for project names and for error text. (Reproduced in the mockup; not in ADE.)

**P5. Lifetime does not follow what the alert says.** T1 stays 15 s after you
answer. Behaviour; see F2.

**P6. No way to dismiss a toast that has a button, and no keyboard path.**
See B9 (close button) and F8.

**P7. The button does not look like a button.** `.contracts-action` on the
card: fill 1.06 to 1.20 : 1 against the card, edge 1.07 to 1.26 : 1, and the
fill is darker than the card, so it reads as an inset field.

**P8. The toast barely separates from what is behind it.** Card against the
terminal background 1.17 to 1.34 : 1; its border (the 10 to 15% white line
over the card's own fill) 1.58 to 2.09 : 1. Hand-written shadow and timing
instead of the product's tokens.

**P9. The words.** T1's title is generic and the useful fact (which tab) is
in the small line. T2 says "this folder" without naming it and does not say
the command is typed, not run. T7, T8, T10 to T12 use a label as a title
("Project setup", "Contracts"). T9 claims "Agent completed its task" from
silence. T13 has no full stop.

**P10. Screen readers.** The `role="status"` node is created with its text
already in it, which is announced unreliably. (Read; no screen reader run.)

**P11. Body text contrast.** `--text-secondary` on the card is 4.22 : 1 in
Solarized Dark (4.5 needed at 12 px).

Not a problem: target size (27 px buttons), title contrast, type sizes.

# Part B. The restyle (the main proposal)

**What does not change:** one alert on screen at a time, and the next
replaces it; every trigger in A1; the three lifetimes (4 s, 15 s, stays); a
click on the body of a card with no button dismisses it; a button runs and
dismisses; the BMAD question has no timer and no dismiss, so it stays until
it is answered or until ADE withdraws it as it does today (the active
terminal leaves that project, or the project gains BMAD:
`useProjectSetup.ts:169-190, 265-269`), and it offers v6 and v4 as equals; `role` politeness (everything polite); the layer (2500), and
with it the fact that the card is drawn over the palette and over dialogs,
as today's toast is; no shortcut; the OS notification attempt, **its words
included** ("`{icon} {agentName}` finished" / "Agent completed its task").

**What changes:** the card; a kind and icon per alert; the words on the
card; safe handling of text ADE did not write; where the card sits; and, if
the owner agrees, a close button (B9).

**Small differences in behaviour that Part B carries.** Draft 2 had these in
their own sections but not here. None changes when an alert fires, how long
it stays or how it is dismissed. Each is kept for the reason given, or
reverted to today's behaviour.

| Difference from today | Now | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A click on a card's button does not move focus into the card (`preventDefault` on `mousedown`); focus that was inside a card returns to where it came from when the card goes (B7) | **Kept** | A click on an alert must not take the cursor out of the terminal. Today it can leave the keyboard on the button, then nowhere once the toast is gone. What the click does is unchanged |
| The announcement starts with the kind, "Needs you: …" (B8) | **Kept** | It is the text form of the new icon; without it a screen reader user gets none of the kind |
| The announcement left out the button labels | **Reverted** | The labels are announced, as today's `role="status"` box announces them. With no key route to a card from a terminal, it is the only place a screen reader user learns there is an "Open tab" button. The T2 hint is announced too |
| A body over 500 graphemes was cut at the end with "…" | **Reverted** (draft 4) | A body is shown whole and announced whole, as today. It was the only difference that removed information, and its reason did not need it: the size rule (B2) already keeps the card inside the window by scrolling the body, where today's toast grows past the top of the window. Nothing else needed the cut: a body has no tooltip, and today's `role="status"` box announces the whole body too |
| **A card can move while it is up** (B2, F13). Today's toast never moves | **Kept**, under A and AB only | Under A and AB the card moves 26 px (28 in a split) when a find bar it would overlap opens or closes. Under AB it changes corner when you switch between a tab that draws a terminal and one that does not. With F13 it changes corner when an overlay opens or closes. Each move keeps the card off a control. The cost: it can move from under a resting pointer, or to under one, as any new alert can land under one today. B and C have none of this |
| A replacement plays the 180 ms entry (B10) | **Kept** | It is what shows that the alert changed. No lifetime or timing changes; none under reduced motion |
| The OS notification took the card's new words | **Reverted** | That surface is out of scope. It keeps today's words (B3) |
| T13's body gained a full stop | **Reverted** | The body is a shared string quoted in the Context Store (B3). Not worth a third contract change |
| The context card had a key in the mockup | **Reverted** | It has none today (`useContextGuard.ts:78-82`). A key comes only with F10 |

## B1. The card and its kinds

`Toast` becomes one `Alert` card. `GuardToast` gains an optional `kind`
(default `note`) and its `title` may be a list of text and name parts.

| Kind | Means | Icon (16 px outline) | Icon colour | Bar | Used by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Needs you** | An agent cannot go on until you act | Ring with a filled dot | red mix | yes | T1 |
| **Question** | ADE is waiting for your answer | Square with "?" | accent mix | yes | T3 |
| **Problem** | Something did not happen | Octagon with "×" | red mix | no | T7 (no journal), T8, T10, T12, T13 |
| **Warning** | Something is not as it should be; acting is optional | Triangle with "!" | yellow mix | no | T2; T4, T5 when attribution is still on; T6 when the archive is unprotected; T7 with a journal |
| **Agent** | An agent stopped, or seems to have. Nothing is known to be required | Circle with two pause bars | neutral (body colour) | no | T9 (and F1) |
| **Note** | ADE did something, or has nothing to do | Square with "i" | accent mix | no | T4, T5, T6, T11 |

- **The bar** is a 3 px strip down the card's left edge in the icon colour.
  It is a shape, not a hue: bar present means "this is waiting for you".
- A kind never rests on colour: each has its own icon outline, and the kind's
  name is the first, visually hidden, words of the title.
- The Agent icon is a pause, not a check, and neutral, not green: neither
  source can know the work is done (MJ6).
- Every kind is derived from what the call site already has (the rule is in
  the "Used by" column), so no new information is needed.

### Anatomy

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────┐
┃ (icon)  [api] needs you              (×)  │  title 13 px / 600, wraps
┃         An agent there can't continue     │  body 12 px / 17 px, wraps anywhere
┃         until you approve or answer       │
┃         something.                        │
┃         [ Open tab ]                      │  buttons 28 px
└───────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

- **Width** 340 px, or the room less 24 px if that is smaller.
- **Padding** 10 / 8 / 10 / 12 px (14 px left with a bar). Icon column 16 px,
  10 px gap. Radius `--radius-lg`.
- **Surface** `--bg-elevated`, `--elev-3` and `--hairline-top`, 1 px outline
  `--alert-edge`.
- **Title** `--text-base`, 600, `--text-primary`. **Body** `--text-sm`,
  colour `--alert-body`.
- **Name box**: a name ADE did not write (tab, project, folder, agent) sits
  in a box inside the title: monospace `--text-sm`, fill `--bg-primary` (the
  terminal's own ground), 1 px outline `--alert-control-edge`, 18 px high.
  The outline is the boundary; no character in the name can draw one.
- **Hint line** (`--text-xs`): only on T2, to say what its button does.
- **Close** (B9): 24 px ghost icon button, top right; last in the DOM.

### New tokens (derived, so all nine presets get them)

```
--alert-control-edge: color-mix(in srgb, var(--text-primary) 60%, var(--bg-elevated))
--alert-red / -yellow / -accent:
                      color-mix(in srgb, var(--red | --yellow | --accent) 60%, var(--text-primary))
```

## B2. Placement

**Anchor.** Three boxes matter:

- the **window**;
- the **content column**: the `minWidth: 320; flex: 1` box in `App.tsx:356`,
  between the file browser and the Preview and Board panels, above the
  scratchpad and shortcut bar. For a terminal tab, the **terminal area** is
  the part of it under the sub-terminal strip and the not-typed bar
  (`#SUB_TERMINAL_CONTENT_ID`). **"Terminal tab", everywhere in this
  document, means a tab for which the terminal layout is what is drawn, not
  `tab.type`.** `App.tsx:357-389` draws the terminal layout for a terminal
  tab and for any tab whose kind lacks its field (an editor tab with no
  file path, a browser tab with no URL, an orchestrator tab with no
  session, a contracts tab with no root). A sub-terminal view and a zoomed
  pane are the terminal layout; the orchestrator tab draws no terminal
  pane;
- the **pane header**, which draft 2 left out. Every terminal pane has one
  over its top edge (`TerminalPane.tsx:122-192`): 4 px down, 8 px in from
  each side, `z-index: 10`, click-through except for one control. It shows
  the pane's folder and, **in the active pane only, the REC button**
  (`RecordingControls.tsx:41-73`), which starts or stops a recording. In the
  mockup REC is 46 × 17 px and ends 21 px under the top of the pane (17 to
  19 px tall with the app's font, so 21 to 23 px). xterm's first row starts
  at 21 px (`padding: 20px 4px 4px 8px` inside 1 px). **In a split** each
  pane also has a 2 px top border (`TerminalPane.tsx:103-106`), so its
  header, REC and find bar sit 2 px lower: REC then ends 23 to 25 px down.

## B5. Text ADE did not write

One function, `cleanAlertText`, applied to **every** name (tab, sub-terminal,
project, folder, agent, task title) and to **every** body, wherever the text
goes: the card, the `title` tooltip, `aria-label`s and the announcement.
Steps, in order:

| Step | What | Code points | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Make well-formed | a lone surrogate becomes U+FFFD | A broken half-character is not text |
| 1 | **Remove** | Every **default-ignorable** code point (Unicode's `Default_Ignorable_Code_Point` property), and U+FFF9 to U+FFFB. That covers draft 2's list (U+00AD, U+061C, U+180E, U+200B, U+200E, U+200F, U+202A to U+202E, U+2060 to U+206F, U+FEFF, U+1D173 to U+1D17A, the tag characters) and what it missed: U+034F, U+17B4, U+17B5, U+1BCA0 to U+1BCA3, U+E0000 to U+E0FFF whole, and the unassigned ones reserved for the same use (U+2065, U+FFF0 to U+FFF8). Three kinds are decided one by one, below | Draws nothing, or changes the direction of the text around it. A property, not a hand-kept list, so the next invisible character Unicode adds is covered |
| 1a | **Joiners stay only where they act** | U+200C and U+200D are kept only **between two letters or marks of a script that uses them** (Arabic, Syriac, Mandaic, Mongolian, N'Ko, Phags-pa, Adlam, Hanifi Rohingya; Devanagari, Bengali, Gurmukhi, Gujarati, Oriya, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Sinhala, Myanmar, Khmer, Tibetan, Balinese, Javanese; by `Script_Extensions`), and U+200D also **between two emoji**. Anywhere else they are removed | "pay", U+200C, "pal" is "paypal". Persian text and emoji families pass unchanged |
| 1b | **Variation selectors stay only after a character** | U+FE00 to U+FE0F, U+E0100 to U+E01EF, U+180B to U+180D and U+180F are kept only straight after a visible character, and only one | An emoji keeps its style; a selector on nothing, or a run of them, goes |
| 2 | **Remove whole** | Terminal escape sequences. **CSI** (`ESC [` or U+009B, its parameter bytes, its final byte; also one cut off before its final byte). **String sequences**: OSC (`ESC ]`, U+009D), DCS (`ESC P`, U+0090), APC (`ESC _`, U+009F), PM (`ESC ^`, U+009E) and SOS (`ESC X`, U+0098), **with their payload, only when the terminator (BEL, `ESC \`, U+009C) comes within 256 characters**. Any other `ESC` pair | So that no "[31m" or "1$r0m" is left behind as text. **Bounded:** a string sequence with no terminator in range loses only its introducer, and its payload stays as text, so an unterminated OSC or a stray U+009D from mis-decoded text cannot swallow the rest of the message |
| 3 | **Replace with a space** | U+0000 to U+001F; U+007F to U+009F; U+2028, U+2029; U+115F, U+1160, U+3164, U+FFA0; U+2800; U+FFFC; U+1D159 | Acts as a break, or draws as a blank without being white space (Hangul fillers, the blank braille cell, the object placeholder, the null notehead). A space keeps the words on each side apart |
| 4 | **Cap marks** | A run of more than four combining marks keeps its first four. A joiner between marks does not restart the count | Stacked marks draw outside the line and over other text |
| 5 | **Cap each grapheme** | No grapheme keeps more than 12 code points | One grapheme can be any length (a chain of joined emoji, of Indic conjuncts, of Hangul lead consonants), so a cap counted in graphemes alone bounds nothing. The longest emoji in Unicode's recommended set is 10 code points (a kiss with two skin tones) |
| 6 | Collapse | Every run of white space (Unicode `\s`) becomes one space; trim | One line, no leading blank |
| 7 | **Nothing visible left** | If what remains has no character other than white space, marks and format characters, it counts as empty | A name of only invisible characters gets the placeholder, not an empty box |

- **Remove or replace is decided once per class** (steps 1 and 2 remove,
  step 3 replaces), so a test can assert it: "a", BEL, "b" is "a b";
  "pay", U+200B, "pal" is "paypal".
- **Empty after cleaning (step 7):** a name reads "Untitled tab", "this
  project", "this folder" or "An agent" (by what it names); a body reads "No
  details were given."
- **Cut.** A name over 24 graphemes keeps its first 13 and last 10 around
  "…" (`Intl.Segmenter`; code points where that is missing). Cutting in the
  middle keeps two names apart when they share a start *or* an end; two that
  differ only in the middle still look alike. The tooltip has the cleaned
  name up to 200 graphemes. **A body is not cut**: it is shown and
  announced whole, as today (draft 3 cut it at 500 graphemes; reverted). The
  size rule keeps the card inside the window.
- **The caps are size bounds.** With step 5, 24 graphemes are at most 288
  code points and 200 at most 2,400. A body has no length cap, but steps 4
  and 5 bound what marks and joiners can add to it: "a" followed by 50,000
  pairs of U+0301 and U+200D comes out as 5 code points, as a name, a
  tooltip and a body alike.
- **Isolated:** a name is a `<bdi>` with `unicode-bidi: isolate`.
- **Boxed:** names in a title. Names inside a body are cleaned, not boxed.
  The box is never narrower than 12 px.
- **Set as text, never as markup** (as now).

Known costs and limits, said plainly:

- The flags of England, Scotland and Wales use tag characters and show as a
  plain black flag.
- A joiner at the end of a word (an older Malayalam or Sinhala spelling) is
  removed, and so is one in a script not on the list: the text shows in its
  default form.
- **A stray U+009B or a lone `ESC` in damaged text takes more than one
  character with it.** U+009B (like `ESC [`) takes everything the CSI
  grammar allows after it: any run of parameter bytes (digits and
  `: ; < = > ?`), then any run of intermediate bytes (**the space** and
  `! " # $ % & ' ( ) * + , - . /`), then one more character. "total",
  U+009B, "1234;5 more" comes out as "totalore". A lone `ESC` takes any run
  of intermediate bytes and one more character: "cost ", `ESC`,
  " 5 dollars" comes out as "cost dollars". The loss is bounded by the
  grammar, not by a length: a run of digits, spaces and punctuation after
  the stray byte goes, however long. The text was already damaged, and
  what is removed is never applied.
- An unterminated OSC leaves its payload as visible text ("Error:
  8;;http://x the rest…"). Ugly, and nothing is hidden.
- A character that draws blank but that Unicode classes as neither
  ignorable, white space nor a format character can still pass. The known
  ones are in step 3. Noncharacters, private-use and unassigned code points
  pass too, drawn as a missing-glyph box or a blank. The 12 px minimum
  keeps the box visible.
- A body is not isolated the way a name is. Strong right-to-left letters in
  a body reorder the digits and punctuation next to them, inside that body
  only; they cannot reach the title or the buttons.

The mockup has a hostile name, a long name, an all-invisible name, and long
and hostile error text, for every alert that carries them. The check script
adds the delta review's hostile inputs (R1 lists them).

## B7. Keyboard and focus

**In the restyle an alert takes no key and no focus.**

- An alert appearing, being replaced, timing out or being withdrawn never
  moves focus.
- **Mouse.** Every button in a card calls `preventDefault` on `mousedown`,
  so a click never moves focus into the card in any engine. Focus stays
  where it was: the terminal, the editor, the scratchpad.
- **Keyboard, with no new shortcut.** The card's buttons are ordinary
  buttons, last in the DOM, so they are in the Tab order after the rest of
  the window, exactly as today's toast buttons are. From an editor or a
  panel, Tab reaches them: action first, then close. From a terminal it does
  not, because xterm keeps Tab. That is today's gap and stays one (F8).
  Enter or Space activates.
- **Focus returns to where it was.** If focus was inside the card when it
  goes (a button was used from the keyboard, or it timed out or was
  replaced), focus returns to the element that had it before it entered the
  card, if that element is still there; otherwise to the active terminal
  (`focusActiveTerminal`). Never to another alert. "Open tab" is the
  exception: focus follows the tab it opens, as now.
- **Esc.** The card has no key handler and stops nothing. Esc with focus on
  a card button does what Esc does anywhere today, in this order:
  1. `useEscapeToClose` (capture phase on `window`): if a panel is open, the
     top one closes and the event stops there.
  2. Otherwise `useKeybindings.ts:161-172`: Settings closes, or the
     scratchpad closes and focus goes to the terminal.

  The card stays. Esc never dismisses an alert in the restyle.

  Each part ends with a full stop unless it already ends in `.`, `?`, `!` or

visible (today only the words change). A card shown again unchanged (the
BMAD question on each `cd`) is the same card and does not replay it. Out:
instant, as today. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`: none.

## B11. Out of the restyle

Stacking, new lifetimes, withdrawal, a shortcut, Esc, assertive

palette or a dialog: all in Part C. OS notifications,
sound, a notification centre, per-kind settings, alerts in detached windows
(`DetachedApp.tsx` mounts no toast): not proposed. M1 to M6 keep their look.

# Part C. Optional follow-ups (behaviour changes)

Each is separate. Each says what changes, the recommendation, the cost, what
"no" leaves, and the conditions it must meet before it is built (the review
findings that applied to it). The mockup's Follow-ups view has a switch for
each, off by default.

## F1. "Agent finished" from Agent status, never doubled

**What changes.** A new card when Claude Code ends a turn, from the
`finished` state ADE already receives. Today's silence guess (T9) stays for
agents that have no such signal.

**The card.** Kind Agent. **`[api]` ended its turn** / The agent there
stopped working. It may be done, or it may have asked you something. /
[Open tab]. 15 s, as any card with a button. It does not say "finished" or
"done": `Stop` is the end of a turn, and Claude Code also ends a turn by
asking you something in plain text.

**Two sources.**

- **H, the hook:** a pane's state becomes `finished`, having been `working`
  or `needsYou` just before, **and stays `finished` for 2 s**; the pane is
  not in view (the active tab of a focused window, as T1). **"In view" is
  read when the 2 s are up, not at `Stop`:** a tab you open during those
  2 s gets no card.
- **S, the silence guess:** today's T9, restyled. Wherever the real state is
  absent, **when it fires, how often, and what it replaces on screen are
  exactly today's**. That is the owner's decision, and rule 5 below does
  not apply to it.

**The rule that makes it one per turn.** Per pane (PTY), ADE reads one
fact, keeps one, and listens for one event:

- **covered** (read, not kept): the pane has an Agent-status state at the
  moment S would speak, that is, its entry in the status store is present.
  It needs no "was launched" fact, and nothing depends on the order of
  `startSession` and the first hook.
- **alerted** (kept): for the pane's current turn, a stop card was shown,
  or was withheld by rule 3 or rule 5.
- **a tracked session started here** (an event): the one "an agent was
  launched in this pane" fact F1 uses, and it uses it only to clear
  **alerted** (rule 4). It comes from the session tracker that exists
  today: `agentTrackerStore.startSession`, which the agent picker, the
  orchestrator and `agentLaunch` call when they start an agent in a pane
  (`AgentPicker.tsx:250`, `OrchestratorTab.tsx:355`, `agentLaunch.ts:91`),
  each straight after the launch command is typed. It is the same tracker
  that makes today's silence guess once per launched session. Nothing new
  is tracked, **covered** does not use it, and the OS notification attempt
  is not touched by it.

Then:

1. **S speaks only if the pane is not covered** and not alerted. Everything
   else that pause does today still happens, covered or not: ending the
   tracked session, marking an orchestrator task completed, and the OS
   notification attempt.
2. **H speaks only if the pane is not alerted.**
3. **alerted is set when a card is shown, and in two cases where one is
   withheld on purpose**: the turn ended in the tab in view (H shows no card
   there, as T1 does not); and rule 5. Nothing else sets it. In particular
   it is not set before these checks are made.
4. **alerted is cleared when a new turn is seen to start, or a new agent is
   launched in the pane.** Four clauses:
   - the pane's state enters `ready`; or
   - it enters `working` from anything but `needsYou` (`working` after
     `needsYou` is the same turn going on); or
   - it enters `needsYou` from `finished` or `waiting` (the agent asks
     something after its turn ended: a turn that starts with a prompt); or
   - **a tracked session starts in the pane** (`startSession`). This is the
     only clause that can act in a pane with no Agent status (Codex,
     Ollama, Claude Code with Agent status off, everything on Windows). It
     keeps S there at once per launched session, as today, and not once
     per pane (case 18).

   **Nothing else clears it.** In particular the pane's state going to
   none does not (the agent exited, or Agent status was turned off), and
   neither does the silence guess ending its tracked session. That is what
   prevents a second card for one turn when Claude Code exits within
   seconds of a carded stop while its tracked session is unspent: the pane
   is then no longer covered, so S is asked, and rule 1 withholds it
   because the pane is still alerted (case 19).
5. **H does not replace another pane's needs-you card.** While one is on
   screen, H's stop is withheld: not shown then, and not shown later. In
   the restyle with F1 alone that is 15 s at most. **That is all rule 5
   covers.**
   - It does **not** cover the BMAD question. H replaces the question, as
     any alert does today, and today's code shows the question again on the
     next `cd` into that project (`useProjectSetup.ts:103-107`). An
     unanswered question therefore cannot hold back stop cards.
   - It does **not** cover S. The silence card replaces whatever is on
     screen, a needs-you card included, exactly as today.
   - A pane's own needs-you card is replaced by its own stop card.
   - **With F4, F5 or F6 the 15 s no longer bounds it**, because each can
     hold a needs-you card's timer. The case that matters is F5 without
     F3: a needs-you card held while ADE is in the background withholds
     every other pane's stop for as long as you are away, and those stops
     are dropped, not shown when you return. F3 removes that.
   - **With F3, rule 5 is off and F3's rule decides:** the newest always
     shows. Where there is room both cards show (case 5). In a slot with
     room for one card the stop card takes the place and the needs-you
     card gives way, as F3 says for any arrival.
   - **With F12, "in view" means for H what it means for T1:** a pane in a
     sub-terminal that is not the shown view is not in view, so its stop
     raises a card. One definition for both cards.

Between two clearings a pane can set **alerted** once, so it gets at most
one card, whichever source saw the stop.

**What marks a stop that got no card.** Not the agent dot: `AgentDot` draws
nothing for `finished` (`AgentDot.tsx:17`). What can show is the attention
dot M2, on the pane's tab, about 3 s after its output stops, and only if
that tab is not the active one (`useTerminal.ts:78-83`, `TabBar.tsx:247`).
A stop in the active tab of an unfocused window leaves no mark at all.

**Cases** (1 to 6, 13 to 19 and the last two rows run in the mockup; the

**What is lost against today, said plainly.**

- **No card at a launched Claude Code agent's first pause, and none for a
  turn that ends in view.** Today T9 shows once per launched session, in
  view or not. (The OS notification attempt at that pause is unchanged.)
- **Esc on a permission prompt** fires no `Stop`, so that turn never reaches
  `finished` and gets no card; S is muted because the pane is covered. Today
  T9 is once per launched session and has usually been spent at the first
  pause, so little is lost in practice, but it is a loss.
- **A killed Claude Code agent** in a covered pane goes to no state, not
  `finished`: no card. Today the silence guess would catch it if unspent.
- **A stale state** left by a killed agent makes the pane count as covered
  until its shell exits. An agent with no hooks launched in that pane then
  gets no silence card.
- **Agent status turned off mid-session.** This is real, not an
  assumption: turning Agent status off for a project drops that project's
  sessions (`agent_status.rs:280-289, 765`), so the pane goes to no state.
  Its one silence guess was spent while it was covered, so nothing more
  comes from that launched session: no stop card (no state) and no silence
  card (session spent).
- **A session first known by a permission request**, in a pane that was
  alerted before, gets no stop card for that turn (cases table).
- **Rule 5:** a stop that arrives while another pane's needs-you card is on
  screen is never shown. At most one per pane for each 15 s such a card is
  up; for longer with F4, F5 or F6, which hold that card's timer.
- **More replacing in a one-slot product.** H adds a card at every turn end
  in the background, and each one replaces what is on screen: an Undo card
  (its Undo goes with it, before its 15 s), an error card, the context
  card, the BMAD question (it returns on the next `cd`). Today only T9 does
  that, once per launched session. F3 is what removes this.

**The OS notification attempt is not part of F1.** It is made exactly as
today: once per launched session, at the first 3 s pause, in view or not,
with today's words and sound (`useTerminal.ts:94-97, 119-126`). F1 adds
none and removes none. Where S is muted for a covered pane the attempt is
still made at that pause; H never makes one. One oddity follows and is left
alone because the surface is out of scope: for a covered pane the OS
notification can say "finished" at a thinking pause where F1 rightly shows
no card.

**Recommendation: yes.** It is the alert the owner named, and the only
source that knows a turn ended.
**Cost:** a rule beside `needsYouToasts` that also takes the previous state
(the store's `subscribe` already supplies it); a subscription to the
session tracker for a session starting in a pane; one read of the status store
in `checkIdleTransition`, placed so that only the in-app card is skipped
(`sendNotification` does both the OS attempt and the in-app event today, so
the two are separated); the latch; a 2 s timer per pane.
**If no:** the product keeps T9 only, restyled and honestly worded ("has
gone quiet"); a `claude` started by hand gets nothing.

**Conditions before building:**

- Keys are per pane (`agent-stopped-{ptyId}`), like T1's; never per tab.
- **Unverified, to check first:**
  - whether Agent status can **begin** mid-session (that it can stop is
    verified: see "What is lost"). Claude Code is believed to read its
    hooks at start; if so, cases 3 and 10 cannot happen and cost nothing;
  - whether hook events can arrive out of order. A late `SubagentStart`
    after `Stop` would show `working` again, clear **alerted**, and could
    give a second card for that turn. Suspected by the last check, not
    checked;
  - whether a turn can start with a prompt and no `UserPromptSubmit` (rule
    4's third clause covers it either way);
  - whether `idle_prompt` fires while a permission prompt is open;
  - whether a user's own blocking Stop hook leaves the pane `finished`
    while the agent works on (the card would then be false).
- `claude -p` run in a loop raises a card per run whenever a run takes more
  than 2 s to exit after `Stop`.
- A pane's state is the most urgent of its sessions. If a `working` session
  is dropped while an older `finished` one remains, the pane shows
  `working` → `finished` with no turn ending, and H speaks. "Stopped
  working" is still true; "ended its turn" is loose. Accept or filter.
- With F2: the stop card is withdrawn when the pane goes `working` or
  `needsYou`, or you open the tab. It **survives** `waiting` (`idle_prompt`,
  about a minute later): the agent is still stopped.

## F2. Take the needs-you card down when it stops being true

**What changes.** The card keeps its 15 s but also leaves early.

| Cause | What happens |
|---|---|
| The pane leaves `needsYou` (answered, turn ended, session gone) | Withdrawn at once |
| You open its tab (the card's button, the tab bar, a shortcut) | Withdrawn at once |
| Its tab comes into view because ADE regained focus | Not withdrawn; it runs out its time, so you see it |
| The state is stale (Esc on the prompt, a wrong guess) or the agent is dead with no pid | Cannot be known; the 15 s timer ends it, as today |
| Its tab is detached to another window | Withdrawn (its button would do nothing) |
| Dismissed | Gone, and not shown again until the pane has left `needsYou` and come back. One such spell can span several queued prompts |

**Recommendation: yes.** Small, and it removes the most visible wrong state.
**Cost:** the hook must also watch the tab store (active tab, tab list), not
only the agent store.
**If no:** the card stays up to 15 s after you answer, as today.

## F3. Up to three cards at once

**What changes.** A newer alert no longer erases the one on screen, while
there is room.

- Up to three cards if the slot has 480 px of height, two from 300 px, else
  one. Newest nearest the anchored edge.
- **The newest always shows**, so the answer to something you just did is
  never hidden. When it does not fit, one card on screen gives way: the
  lowest in this order first (Note, Agent, Warning, Problem, Needs you,
  Question), then the oldest.
- **A card that gives way is dropped.** There is no queue, no "N more" line
  and no history, so it is not shown again. "Nothing waits out of sight" is
  true only because of that. F3 does not end alerts being lost unseen; it
  makes it rarer and chooses which one goes.
- **When a needs-you card can be pushed out:** only when a card arrives,
  the slot is full, and no Note, Agent, Warning or Problem card is on
  screen to give way first. So: three needs-you cards and any fourth alert,
  and the oldest needs-you goes; or a slot with room for one card, and any
  arrival. Three Notes cannot push out a needs-you card while a lower card
  is there to go. Its tab keeps the red dot while the agent still needs
  you, so the fact is not lost, only the card.
- **The BMAD question gives way last:** only when it is the only other card
  on screen (a slot with room for one). It is then shown again on the next
  `cd`, as today. It never holds up the newest. (Draft 2 said both that it
  "never gives way" and that it "cannot hold up anything else". In a
  one-card slot only one of those can be true; this is the one.)
- Four alerts raised in one tick, with room for three: the first is never
  painted, though it is announced.
- The same key updates its card in place. Keys are per pane, never per tab.
- Each alert is announced when raised; texts in one tick are appended, not
  overwritten.

**Recommendation: yes, after F1 and F2.** It fixes "Not sent" erasing "needs
you" one second later, and three agents at once showing one. F1 fills the
slot at every turn end and F2 empties it when a card stops being true, so
F3's rule is tuned against both and waits for them.
**An option to decide when F3 is built:** bring a needs-you card that gave
way back when a place frees, if its pane still needs you. It can be rebuilt
from the state, so it is not a queue. Not designed here.
**Cost:** the largest follow-up: the single state becomes a list, a timer
per card, `withdrawToast` becomes a list filter, the height rule needs
measuring.
**If no:** one card; the newest wins, as today.

## F4. Time holds under the pointer; errors get reading time

A card's timer stops while the pointer is over it, and a Problem with no
button stays 8 s, not 4. **Recommendation: yes.** Cost: small (a pausable
timer). If no: 4 s for every error, whatever its length.

## F5. Time holds while ADE is in the background

Timers stop while the window is not focused, and a held card gets at least
4 s after you return. **Recommendation: yes, with F2.** T1 is raised exactly
when the window may be unfocused, so today it can come and go unseen.
Conditions: the stop card must survive `waiting` (F1); with F2 a card is not
withdrawn the instant focus returns. **A cost if F1 is built and F3 is
not:** a needs-you card held while you are away withholds every other
pane's stop card for that whole time, and those stops are dropped (F1
rule 5). If no: alerts raised while you are away
may be gone when you come back; the tab dots remain.

## F6. Needs-you stays until answered (no timer)

**Recommendation: no.** `needsYou` errs toward still showing, and a killed
agent with no pid holds it until its shell exits. Today a false needs-you
costs 15 s; with no timer it would sit there saying an agent cannot continue
when no prompt is open. If the owner wants it anyway, it needs F2, a close
button and a cap (for example 5 minutes).

## F7. A click on the body never dismisses

Only with the close button (B9). One rule for every card.
**Recommendation: yes if B9 is yes.** If no: buttonless cards still vanish on
a stray click, as today.

## F8. A keyboard route to alerts

**What changes.** A palette command, "Alerts: Focus the alert", moves focus
to the card's first button, and back when used again. Esc with focus inside
a card dismisses it (on the question it only returns focus). Optionally a
shortcut.

**Recommendation: yes.** Without it a keyboard user in a terminal cannot
reach a card.
**Conditions:**

- **The key is the architect's to pick, and not ⌘⇧L** (Monaco binds it to
  select-all-occurrences, and ADE has editor tabs). It must be free in
  `src/lib/shortcuts.ts`, in Monaco's defaults and in common terminal
  programs. The palette command alone is enough to ship.
- **Esc order.** The card handles Esc only while focus is inside it, in the
  capture phase, registered so that it runs before `useEscapeToClose`'s
  entry, and stops the event, so it neither closes a panel nor reaches
  `useKeybindings` (which would close the scratchpad).
- Focus returns to the element that had it, not "the terminal".

**If no:** cards stay mouse-first from a terminal, as today.

## F9. Announce the failure of something you just did at once

A second region (`role="alert"`), mounted at launch. Politeness is chosen
**by cause, not kind**: assertive only when the alert answers a command the
user gave in this window just now. That needs a flag from the sender: T8,
T7 from the palette, New tab or the board, T10 to T12. Not T7 from the `cd`
hook, and T13 only when its caller says the write was the user's.
**Recommendation: yes.** Cost: the flag at about eight senders. If no:
everything stays polite.

## F10. The context card leaves when its button would miss

Its button types into the active terminal, whichever that is by then. The
card is withdrawn when the active terminal changes folder, **and when the
tab in front stops being a terminal** (the button would then type nothing
and say nothing).
**Recommendation: yes.** Cost: one `withdraw` by key in `useContextGuard`
(the card gets a key for this; the one-slot `withdrawToast` is enough). If
no: the title names the folder and the hint says "the active terminal", so
the card is honest; the button can still type `/compact` somewhere else, or
nothing at all.

## F11. "Use v6" as the suggested answer

A product decision, not styling. `useProjectSetup.ts:140-143` offers the two
as equals. The label would read **Use v6 (suggested)** and be filled; the
words carry it, because filled against outlined is hue-only in Solarized
Dark. **Recommendation: the owner's to make.** If no: two equal buttons.

## F12. Name and open the sub-terminal

Already specified (sub-terminals UX, 2026-11-02, "Agent-status toasts") and
not built. Title **`[api]` › `[npm]` needs you**, each name in its own box so
a tab named "api › npm" cannot imitate it; the button reads "Open
sub-terminal" and shows that view. Also: a pane in a sub-terminal that is not
the shown view is **not** in view, so it raises a card.
**Recommendation: yes.** If no: the card names and opens the tab only.

## F13. A top-right card steps aside for the palette or a dialog

Only matters if Q1 is A or AB. While the command palette, the agent picker,
the recording player or a dialog is open, a card that would hang at the top
right uses B's place, and goes back when the overlay closes.

- It takes the card off the palette's input and off a dialog's close button
  (B2's second table). In B's place it can still lie on the right 33 px of
  the footer of a dialog at its tallest, as today's toast does.
- **Cost:** every overlay has to say it is open. About twelve components:
  the palette, the agent picker and the recording player set their layer
  inline, the rest share `.ui-overlay` or have their own class. One shared
  count or one marker class. The card also moves when an overlay opens,
  under the pointer if the pointer is on it.

**Recommendation: yes if Q1 is A or AB.** If no: a card on a terminal tab
covers what B2's second table says, the BMAD question until it is answered
or withdrawn.

## Not proposed

A "Not now" on the BMAD question (F3's rule means it blocks nothing);
removing or changing the OS notification attempt; a minimum window size.
